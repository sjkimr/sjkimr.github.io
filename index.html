01

Research interests

Bimanual Manipulation

Manipulation with two arms, on one object together or on different objects separately. The main questions are which arm should grasp where, what each arm should do, and in what order, on dual-arm manipulators and humanoids.

  • Dual-arm grasping
  • Arm assignment
  • Long-horizon tasks

Robot & Imitation Learning

Policies that generalize beyond their training demonstrations. I am interested in how imitation learning and vision-language-action models behave on real robots, and in the gap between what a model understands and what the robot actually does.

  • Imitation learning
  • VLA models
  • Teleop data

Robot Perception

Perception for manipulation: detecting and segmenting objects, estimating where they are and how they can be grasped, and understanding what is around them from RGB-D images and 3D point clouds.

  • Detection & segmentation
  • Grasp affordance
  • 6D pose
  • Point clouds

03

Projects

  1. 01 2026.10

    Agent-driven bimanual manipulation

    An LLM agent drives a dual-arm Franka system through a long-horizon kitchen task with no task-specific training.

    • LLM agent
    • Franka ×2
    • 3-finger gripper
    • RGB-D
    • Python
    Details

    A general-purpose LLM agent acts as the policy for a dual-arm Franka system. One sentence of instruction becomes a zero-shot, long-horizon task: the agent defines the task, runs an observe–decide–act–verify loop, keeps lessons in cross-session memory, and recovers from its own failures.

    RESULT 4 / 5 success on "put the apple in the pot and move it to the stove" (open lid → pick → place → close lid → carry pot with both arms) with no task-specific training.

  2. 02 2026.03 — now Ongoing

    Humanoid VLA on the Unitree G1

    Teleoperation data pipelines and GR00T fine-tuning on a real Unitree G1 humanoid.

    • GR00T
    • SONIC
    • Teleoperation
    • PICO VR
    • Unitree G1
    • PyTorch
    Details

    Built teleoperation data pipelines for the Unitree G1 — DYNAMIXEL leader arms for upper-body manipulation, NVIDIA SONIC + PICO VR for whole-body control — and fine-tuned GR00T to probe compositional generalization on pick-and-place, fridge opening, and grasping on the real robot.

    RESULT 640+ teleop episodes (320 upper-body pick-and-place over 8 objects, 324 whole-body for two skills); GR00T policies deployed autonomously on the real G1.

  3. 03 2025.01 — 2026.03 IROS 2026

    BiGraspAfford: bimanual grasp affordance learning

    Separate left- and right-arm grasp affordances so a dual-arm robot knows which arm grasps where.

    • PyTorch
    • VLM
    • Point clouds
    • 6D grasp pose
    • Franka ×2
    Details

    Predicts a separate grasp affordance for the left and the right arm, so a dual-arm robot knows which arm should grasp where while accounting for collisions with the scene. A VLM grounds the instruction and picks arms and skills from L/R visual prompts; a sampling-based MPC controller executes. Joint research with the Hyundai Motor Robotics Lab.

    RESULT 86% success on real-robot bimanual tasks, 29% shorter execution than arm-agnostic grasping, and +28 / +37 mAP over 3DAffordanceNet on seen / unseen objects.

  4. 04 2024.01 — 2025.01 Industry

    Real-time image enhancement for remote robot operation in hazardous sites

    Real-time super-resolution and contrast enhancement on a Jetson Nano for robots at furnace sites.

    • PyTorch
    • ESPCN
    • CLAHE
    • OpenCV
    • Jetson Nano
    Details

    Remote operators of furnace-site robots could not see slag regions against the glare of molten metal. A custom ESPCN super-resolution model with residual learning, combined with CLAHE for backlight contrast, restores detail in real time on an embedded Jetson Nano. Commissioned by POSCO Holdings.

    RESULT PSNR 30.59 dB, a 9.6% gain over the baseline ESPCN, running in real time on a Jetson Nano.

  5. 05 2021.07 — 2024.02 RoboCup

    Integrated vision system for humanoid robot soccer

    A real-time detection, tracking, and localization pipeline for RoboCup humanoid soccer, running on the robot's on-board PC.

    • C/C++
    • OpenCV
    • YOLOv4-tiny
    • ROS
    • Qt
    • PID control
    • Particle filter
    Details

    RoboCup humanoid soccer is fully autonomous: the robot has to find the ball, goals, lines, and other robots, know where it stands on the field, and walk there by itself. I built the vision stack end to end, from camera calibration to the ball position and robot pose that the behavior layer consumes.

    Detection. White lines and penalty marks come from color-space thresholding (HSV, LAB, or YUV chosen per object), field masking, and contour labeling. The ball and X-crosses come from a YOLO-tiny detector trained on field images we collected and labeled ourselves. Moving inference from YOLOv3-tiny on Darknet to YOLOv4-tiny on OpenCV DNN took on-board throughput from 6.25 to 24 fps, fast enough to keep detecting while walking.

    Tracking and distance. A PID loop on the pan–tilt head keeps the ball centered in the image during gait. Camera intrinsics combined with the robot height and head angles project the ball pixel onto the ground plane for range and bearing, which drive the approach walk. With our own calibration the distance error at 1.5–2 m came down to 11.6%, from 23.7% with the stock ROS calibration tool.

    Localization. Line contours, penalty marks, and X-crosses feed a particle filter that estimates the robot's pose on the field. An in-house Qt tool shows the live camera, the HSV thresholds, and the particle map side by side for tuning at the venue.

    RESULT Six podium finishes in 2021–2022, including 2nd place in the RoboCup 2022 KidSize Technical Challenge.
